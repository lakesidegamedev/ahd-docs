# The commodity exchange

Goods trade on one exchange that spans the whole scenario. Every company's need for an input, in any region, meets every seller's stock in any region and the import offer of the buyer's own region. Offers are priced as delivered, the asking price plus freight over the lane, and filled cheapest first, so local producers supply local plants and the outside market is the last resort.

## Prices

- The outside market (the **gateways**) imports and exports at world prices plus or minus a band.
- A good sells out and its price rises only while the region is still importing it. When local supply covers demand the price eases back toward its reference. Local sellers stay under the cheapest import price, so local goods win once freight is added.
- Your goods follow the same sell-through rule as rivals unless you set the price by hand.

## World prices

Oil, natural gas, coal, ore, steel, grain, fertilizer, chemicals, timber, rare earths and building materials have a world price that moves every week and sets what gateways charge to import and pay to export. The path is chosen at new game: Historical, Historical with variance, or Random. A collapse in world prices feeds the business cycle as a demand shock.

## Chains

Plants can make several products at once. Oil is a real chain: wells pump crude oil and refineries turn it into fuel, gasoline, jet fuel and feedstock. Iron runs from blast furnace to pig iron to steel, with scrap from steel works and car plants feeding electric arc furnaces. Mixed farms grow grain and raise livestock, and food processing turns both into food.

## Storage

Goods take up space. Every plant has a working yard; anything beyond it sits in outside storage for a weekly fee. You can build warehouses, tank farms, grain silos and cold stores, earn fees from other firms' overflow, and cut spoilage of food, grain and livestock. **Buy to store** lets you buy a good on the exchange into storage and release it later, to ride a price swing.

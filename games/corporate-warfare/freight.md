# The freight network

Freight is lane capacity. Goods move between regions by trucking, rail, pipeline and lake or sea shipping. Each mode has its own price, speed, smallest load, terminal needs and list of goods it may carry: pipelines take only liquids and gas, and cold goods go by truck or rail.

## Owning carriers

Carrier divisions give your group a fixed weekly capacity on the lanes they serve. You ship on your own carriers first and at cost. Other shippers pay a rate that rises as the lane fills, and outside carriers take the overflow at a premium, so goods never stop. Rail terminals and ports can be built and open their region to every firm.

## Shipping policy

Each division has a standing shipping policy: cheapest, fastest, own carriers first or most reliable, with an optional preferred mode or carrier. The engine routes every shipment over up to three legs.

## Lane contracts

A lane contract reserves capacity for 4 to 52 weeks at a locked rate. You can use one to lock rivals off a lane you depend on, and rivals can do the same to you. Under common carrier rules, owned pipelines, rail and ships must sell spare capacity at a capped rate and may not lock a lane up with contracts.

## Disruptions

Pipeline spills, port strikes, refinery fires, lane outages and harvest failures hit the network with real durations and effects on lanes, plants and stock. Alerts appear only for lanes you ship on: a lane full for weeks, a carrier rate rise or a rival reserving a lane. The map's freight layer colours lanes by congestion.

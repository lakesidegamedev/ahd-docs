# Careers and ambitions

A career gives your company a start, a goal and an ending. You choose all three on the New game screen, and the game tells you every quarter how close you are. This page covers the starts, the four ambitions, what bronze, silver and gold take, and how a career ends.

## How it works

### Choosing a start

New career runs in five steps: scenario, how you start, ambition, world and chief. You can start in one of two ways:

- **Take the helm of an established company.** The game offers three companies from the middle of the league, in different industries where it can. Each shows its rank, sector, value, a year's sales, plants, workers, the price of control and the fortune the start gives you. You buy a controlling block from the company's outside shareholders at the opening share price, so they are paid and nothing is made out of thin air. If your usual starting fortune cannot pay for control, it is raised to just cover the block, and the game says so. The old chief executive steps down, the board stays, and from the first week the company is yours.
- **Found your own company.** Name it (the game checks the name in plain words: not empty, no line breaks, at most 40 characters), pick a headquarters region and a first strategy, and start from nothing.

A game started with no career choices plays as a sandbox, the way every earlier version played.

### Choosing an ambition and a length

You choose what you play for, and over 5, 10 or 20 years. Each ambition has bronze, silver and gold.

| Ambition | What it asks | Bronze, silver, gold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Richest | Grow your own fortune | Over ten years in Great Lakes: 17, 25 and 40 times your starting fortune. Texas: 11, 16 and 25. Silicon Valley: 4.5, 8 and 15. Ruhr and Rhine: 8.3, 15 and 25 |
| Biggest | Run the largest company | A founder earns bronze in the top half of the league, silver in the top ten and gold in the top three. If you took the helm you need the top ten, the top three and first place |
| Dominant | Make an industry yours | 20, 30 and 40 percent of its output, held for four quarters in a row |
| Survivor | Keep a solvent company | Silver in the top half of the league, gold in the top quarter |

Five and twenty year careers scale the Richest targets the same way. When you take the helm, growth counts from what you own just after buying control. The New game screen shows the Great Lakes figures for Richest, and the career screen during play shows the ones for your scenario. Medal wording and lengths come from the game's own content.

### Where you stand

Every quarter the game tells you where you stand and what the next medal needs. The line also appears in the weekly brief. The Career page shows your ambition, medal, rank, what the next medal needs, the board's warning and a year by year record. The Hall of Careers keeps your finished careers.

### How a career ends

A career can be lost at any time:

- you have no company and too little money to found one;
- a rival takes control of your company;
- your board removes you after a long run of losses.

The board warns you first, and a profitable month restores its confidence. A board you control cannot remove you: while you hold half or more of the votes it can only warn you, and the market does the rest through your credit, your share price, activist shareholders and your lenders.

When a career ends, a full screen shows the medal and the ambition, a one sentence verdict, the year by year record and the moments that mattered. It offers three ways on: start a new career, keep playing in this world, or go to the main menu. You can open it again from Career and from the Hall of Careers.

## What you can do

- **Take the helm first.** It is the easier start, because the company already has plants, staff and customers.
- **Pick an ambition that fits your style.** Richest rewards growth and good deals, Biggest rewards expansion, Dominant rewards owning one industry and Survivor rewards staying solvent through bad years.
- **Watch the quarterly line.** It names the next medal and what it takes.
- **Keep your votes.** Holding half or more of your own company's votes is what stops the board removing you.

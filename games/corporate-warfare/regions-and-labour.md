# Regions and labour

## Regions

The map is divided into economic regions, each built from whole counties or districts around an anchor city. A region is the economic unit. It has its own:

- labour pool and wages,
- household demand,
- land cost,
- goods markets,
- freight connections.

The state or country above it is the political layer: conditions such as the power grid, roads, workforce skill and crime, union law and taxes. Some states are specialized in two sector types, and plants of those types use fewer inputs there.

Plants managed from outside their company's home region pay a little more for inputs, and more again outside the home state. Goods, retail, leases and electricity clear in their own region; services that reach customers statewide, such as software, telecoms, advertising and construction, clear across the state.

## Labour

Every region has its own worker pools by skill tier. Firms, rivals and background employers all hire from the same pools by offering wages:

- A small share of workers looks for a new job every week. Job seekers go to the offers that pay most above what they would accept, so a low offer draws few applicants.
- The going wage in a region drifts toward what employers are offering, a little each week.
- A plant runs only as hard as its least-staffed skill tier allows. Staff you are not using still draw wages.
- Mothballing a plant releases its whole workforce back to the pool. Reopening rehires through the labour market.

Farms run seasonal crews. A government can set a low-skill wage floor and union law, and wages and demand follow the business cycle.

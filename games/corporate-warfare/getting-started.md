# Getting started

This page takes you from download to a first career that is running well. The short version: install the game, start in Great Lakes 2000 on Standard difficulty, take over the running company, leave prices on automatic, and spend your first ten weeks learning the screens before you spend money.

## Offline play, saves and updates

The game is single-player and plays fully offline once installed. Saves stay on your device. Use Save in the in-game menu to keep a save beside the autosave. Load is a save browser: every save in a grid you can sort by name, scenario, game date, net worth, medal, play time, version or when it was saved, with Rename, Duplicate, Export, Import and Delete. Saves from every earlier version still open, and a game started without a career stays a sandbox.

The launcher keeps the game up to date. When the launcher starts the game, the game's own update checks switch off and Settings, Updates says so. If you are signed in to the launcher, the main menu and Settings show "Signed in as" your name. The game never checks that you own it while you play. If you start the game without the launcher, it can tell you when a newer version is out and, once you sign in to Lakeside, download it through your library with a checksum check. The first time you open the main menu after an update, a What's new panel lists the top five changes.

The game can also send an anonymous market snapshot every five game weeks: prices, sales and stock per good, with a random install id and nothing about you. It is never linked to your account, and the Analytics switch in Settings turns it off.

## Your first career

The game opens on the main menu: Continue (with the last save's company, scenario, game date, net worth, ambition, medal and time played), New career, Load, Settings and, on desktop, Quit. Scenarios shows the four with their start year and what makes each different.

New career runs in five steps with Back and Next always in the same place, and Quick start on every step:

1. **Scenario.** Pick one of the [four scenarios](scenarios.md), or a custom scenario you made in the scenario editor. For a first game, take Great Lakes, 2000: the fewest rivals, one currency and a plain start with a farm in central Ohio.
2. **How you start.** Take the helm of one of three established companies, or found your own. See [Careers and ambitions](careers.md).
3. **Ambition.** Richest, Biggest, Dominant or Survivor, over 5, 10 or 20 years.
4. **World.** Difficulty (table below), the economy on day one (Historic, Calm, Boom or Downturn) and the world price path (Historical, Historical with variance or Random). See [The commodity exchange](commodity-exchange.md).
5. **Chief.** Your company name and your chief executive.

| | Easy | Standard | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Your starting capital | $8,000,000 | $5,000,000 | $3,000,000 |
| Rivals | Never attack your markets | Cost leaders, consolidators and vultures fight for share | Every rival fights for share |

Difficulty changes starting money and how aggressive rivals are. It does not change prices or rules. The starting capital for each difficulty comes from the game. Losing a company does not end a career by itself; see [Careers and ambitions](careers.md) for what does.

A guided tutorial of eight steps ticks off as you do them, each with a Show me button: open the CEO Office, play a week, set a price, find an action in the Command Center, fund research, read the economy card, build a plant and check Stats. You can restart it from Settings.

## The first ten weeks

A practical opening for a sandbox game in Great Lakes, where you run a five-block farm in central Ohio. If you took the helm of another company the figures differ, but the habits are the same:

1. **Week 0.** Open the CEO Office. Read cash ($1,000,000), the loan (about $4,800 of principal due each week) and what the farm makes and sells. Leave every price on automatic. Automatic pricing follows sell-through and never moves a price more than 2 percent a week.
2. **Weeks 1 to 4.** Press Next week, or set the speed to 1x and let time run. Watch stock and cash. Farm output follows the season (80, 110, 130, 80 percent of full by quarter), so low output early in a quarter is normal.
3. **Weeks 2 to 6.** Open Research and set a small weekly budget. Research that costs more than the company earns is a loss.
4. **Weeks 4 to 8.** Open the Map and look for a region with room to build, meaning it imports what you could sell. Adding a plant to a division you already run has no entry fee. Opening a division in a new region costs a $100,000 fee and the founding plant is half price.
5. **Week 10.** Open Stats. Check that revenue exceeds costs and that cash is not falling faster than the loan repayments. Cash running low is the first thing that sinks a company.

Pause when the Inbox shows a decision. The game stops for choices that need you.

## The screens and the table layout

Version 2.0 has a new layout built around tables you can shape. On desktop it is the default, and the classic five tab layout stays under Settings, Classic layout, for this release.

A strip along the top always shows cash, profit, cash in four weeks, the share price, your place in the league by market value, net worth, the economy, the date and the time controls, and each figure opens the page behind it. Pages are grouped by what you run (Company, Markets, Rivals, World, You). The map docks beside any page with Ctrl M, Ctrl K opens the actions palette and finds places, goods, companies, divisions, saved views and actions, and a rail on the right lists only things you can act on (Needs you, Watch and Feed).

Every table works the same way:

- Click a heading to sort, and again to reverse. Pick which columns to show, and show a figure as its change, change in percent, trend, bar or rank.
- One control bar in the same order: View, Scope, Timeframe, Search, Filter, Columns and a menu for the rest. Scope narrows a table to your own rows, a sector or a region.
- Each screen has ready views (for example Goods: Overview, Prices, Supply, My business, Trade). You can save your own under a name. Saved views are kept in your settings, not in your saves, so they carry to your next career.
- The timeframe in the top strip (4 weeks, 13 weeks, a year, five years or everything) drives every change, percent, trend and bar. The tables reach back five years where the game keeps history.
- Type terms such as `sector:chemicals price>10 mine` in the search and press Enter to filter.
- Goods and companies can be starred, and a starred item can carry one alert (price above or below a level).

On a phone the layout has a bottom bar with Map, Company, Markets, Rivals and More, and the same tables become two line rows with the same sort and filter. On iPad and Mac, Plants and Finance (beside the Office) and Goods, Rivals and Regions (under Markets) are sortable tables like the desktop game. The iPhone keeps its own screens for now.

The 3D map is the default map on every platform, and you can zoom in far enough to see each plant's model. The classic flat map stays in Settings, Graphics, "3D map", for computers with no usable graphics card. Double-tap the map on a phone to zoom in and tap with two fingers to zoom out.

Keys on desktop: Space plays or pauses, N plays the next week, 1, 2 and 3 set the speed to 1, 2 or 4 weeks a second, and F1 to F9 open the pages of the classic layout. Settings, Controls and keys lets you change the clock and page keys; Esc, Ctrl K and Ctrl M stay fixed. On Mac, Command K, Command N and Command P do the same.

## Common questions

- **How do I win?** Pick an ambition and aim for bronze, silver or gold (see [Careers and ambitions](careers.md)). A game started without a career is a sandbox with a score out of 100 at week 260 (see [Overview](overview.md)).
- **Which scenario is easiest?** Great Lakes, 2000.
- **How do I pause or speed up?** Pause, 1x, 2x and 4x buttons on the status bar; keys above.
- **Can I recover from bankruptcy?** A company can be rescued, see [Your holding company](companies.md). A career is lost if you have no company and too little money to found one, so see [Careers and ambitions](careers.md).

# Currencies

A scenario may span several countries, each with its own money and central bank. Ruhr and Rhine, 1991 is the first scenario that uses this. Scenarios with one currency play exactly as before.

## Books

Every firm keeps its books in the money of its home region. The engine converts at the current rate wherever money crosses a currency line: trade, freight, the gateways, taxes, outside loans, share issues and dividends.

## Exchange rates

Each week the rates move with the countries' relative inflation, their central bank rates and a random shock. A currency can be pegged to another inside a band, like the 1991 European exchange rate mechanism. A strained peg can break and its band widens, as in 1992 and 1993 when the scenario follows its historical path. Gateway prices and wages follow each country's money.

Sales paid in another currency are collected two weeks later at the rate of the day, and the difference is booked as a foreign exchange gain or loss.

## Limits

- Contracts, loans, deposits, insurance, joint ventures, mergers and raids stay inside one currency.
- A firm builds only in regions that use its money. To operate in another country, found a firm there.
- Only firms keeping books in the lead currency hold stakes in other firms and issue bonds, and the exchange quotes shares in that currency.

## Reporting currency

You see your net worth and cash in a reporting currency you choose. Company figures carry their own currency where it differs, and the economy card has a foreign exchange section.

# Polls and scoring

## Polls

Each state's poll shows the two-party share, and the margin is the lead between the two main candidates. Small shifts near an even race count for more than the same shift in a state that is already lopsided. The trends screen shows national polling, electoral votes and momentum by week, plus a poll line for each state.

## Electoral votes

In a US campaign every state is winner-take-all except Maine and Nebraska, which award votes by congressional district. The bar at the top of the screen tracks the live projection. 270 electoral votes wins.

Other countries are decided by their own systems: seats in the UK and Canada, Bundestag seats in Germany, a runoff in France, and preferences in Australia.

## Turnout

Each bloc has a base likelihood of voting. Enthusiasm from rallies, events and momentum raises it, and get-out-the-vote multiplies it where you have field offices. Blocs that usually stay home have the most to gain.

## What moves the polls

- Advertising, depending on how well it fits the bloc, with diminishing returns.
- Rallies, through momentum and enthusiasm, with a gaffe risk.
- Events, often national.
- Issue pivots, which realign blocs at a trust cost.
- Opposition research, which lands a scandal or backfires.
- Ground game and get-out-the-vote, which change turnout rather than opinion.

## The result

When the campaign ends, the results screen explains why you won or lost before it lists the states. From there you can share the result or move on to the next election.

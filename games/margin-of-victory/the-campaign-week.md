# The campaign week

Each turn is one week of the campaign, running up to election day. A turn has four parts:

1. **Your plan.** Queue actions across the seven days of the week. You can undo anything before you end the turn.
2. **The opponent.** The AI campaign acts according to the difficulty: where it targets, how much it spends, how it answers events.
3. **Events.** Random and scheduled events arrive. Some ask you to decide; others apply on their own.
4. **Resolution.** Polls update, momentum shifts and resources refresh. The week-in-review shows what moved and why.

## Plan bonuses

Actions resolve in order from day 1 to day 7, so order matters. Setting up a move early in the week and following through later makes it stronger. A rally before advertising in the same place gives the ads +20%, and field offices built before a get-out-the-vote push make the push work.

## What limits you

- **Cash.** Spent on ads, surrogates, field offices, get-out-the-vote and opposition research. Fundraising brings more in.
- **Actions.** A weekly pool of 7 plus your candidate's energy divided by 20 (rounded up), plus any running mate bonus and a difficulty bonus (Easy +5, Normal +1). Every move costs one action, with at most 3 per day. The pool refills each week.
- **Staff capacity.** A hard cap on how many field offices you can run. It does not refill.
- **Momentum.** National and per state. It drives poll movement and makes fundraising easier. It can turn negative.

This page describes the US campaign. Other countries use the same weekly loop with their own maps and electoral rules.
